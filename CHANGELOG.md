# Changelog

## Unreleased

- NEW: Heights in metres from a terrain model. OpenStreetMap levels are counted from each building's own ground floor, so on a slope the levels of two buildings do not meet. With a terrain model, given as `dem` (a raster file such as a GeoTIFF, a list of tiles, or a function `(longitudes, latitudes) -> heights`), pyrosm3d places the walking network of an indoor read at heights in metres. `osm.in3d.get_network(dem)` returns the nodes and the edges with the columns `u_height` and `v_height`, the height at each end of an edge, and with lines that have a height at every vertex; `pyrosm3d.network_heights(edges, dem)` does the same for edges that you have read yourself. `osm.in3d.plot_network(dem=...)` and `pyrosm3d.plot_network(..., dem=...)` draw the network, a graph and a route in metres, with the height of each point in its hover text. A street follows the terrain model. An end of another edge at a street's node has the ground's height there. The other ends are found together: an edge on one level keeps its ends at one height, an edge between two levels, such as stairs, an escalator or a lift, wants 4 m for each level, and tunnels, bridges and indoor ways without a level run straight between the streets at their ends. OpenStreetMap holds no depths, so a floor that meets no street, such as a metro platform, is placed 4 m per level from the floors that it is joined to, and what nothing joins to a street 4 m per level from the ground at its place. Where the terrain model has no height, the height of an end is NaN, its line stays flat and the figure leaves the edge out. Area outlines cannot be drawn together with `dem`. Reading a raster file needs rasterio, which the new extra `pyrosm3d[dem]` installs; pyrosm3d bundles and downloads no terrain data. The documentation has a page with an example from the centre of Helsinki. ([#39](https://github.com/pyrosm/pyrosm3D/pull/39), [#40](https://github.com/pyrosm/pyrosm3D/pull/40), [#41](https://github.com/pyrosm/pyrosm3D/pull/41), [#42](https://github.com/pyrosm/pyrosm3D/pull/42))
- NEW: The first version of pyrosm3d draws the OpenStreetMap elements that pyrosm reads with their levels in 3D, with plotly. It gives pyrosm's `OSM` object the accessor `in3d`, whose views read what they draw with pyrosm (only `plot_network` with a graph it is given reads nothing): the indoor elements with the walking areas (`get_indoor_elements(walking_areas=True)`), the walking network with `indoor_elements=True` and the buildings. `osm.in3d.plot_indoor(level=None)` draws the indoor elements as a figure: floors 4 m apart, open where stairs, an escalator or a moving walkway rises through them; walls with a gap at each door, cut away at 1.6 m; the outlines of levels, of buildings that hold indoor areas and of station buildings; outer walls along level or building outlines; stairwells and lift shafts as columns; the walking network; and doors and shops as markers. The areas are the indoor areas, the platform areas, the walking areas, such as squares and station halls, and the other areas that lines of the walking network cross, each with a kind (room, corridor, other indoor area, shaft, toilets, shop, platform, walking area, level outline, wall) and its levels; every kind but the level outlines and the walls has a floor. An area whose only level lies between two floors, such as a mezzanine tagged `level=0.5`, is drawn at its own height, and the web page has a level button for it. `osm.in3d.to_html(path)` writes the figure as a web page with a button for each level, which names the number of areas on it, and a panel that says what a clicked element is and lists its OpenStreetMap tags. `osm.in3d.plot_network()` draws the walking network alone, each edge at its levels, also a graph of `get_graph(indoor_elements=True)` with a route (an edge of a simplified graph that joins several ways is drawn way by way, each part with the tags of its way); it is pyrosm's unreleased `plot_3d`, moved here, and draws moving walkways in a colour of their own. The functions `plot_indoor(elements, edges, nodes, buildings, level=None)`, `to_html(elements, path, edges, nodes, buildings)` and `plot_network(edges, nodes)` draw frames that were read with pyrosm, also after they were changed: the indoor elements (walls, doors, and the shops and amenities that have a level among them), the walking network and the building outlines. Shown as HTML (the output of a notebook cell, a documentation page, a file written with `fig.write_html()`), the figures of `plot_indoor` and `plot_network` have the page's controls for touch screens: two fingers zoom and move the view, touches on the view do not scroll the page, the view stays as it was turned when a tool of plotly's toolbar is picked, and on a small screen the legend is hidden at start, with a button in the figure that shows it over the view. `plot_network` keeps the station entrances as a legend entry that is switched off at start. pyrosm3d needs a pyrosm that has `OSM.in3d`
